Cross-cultural comparison is most useful when it distinguishes three levels: a shared question, a structural analogy and an identical scientific theory. Indian philosophical systems and modern physics can share questions about unity, change, observation and cosmic origin without sharing equations or empirical predictions.

## Vedānta and quantum theory

Vedānta is a family of interpretations, not one doctrine. Advaita Vedānta, for example, distinguishes ultimate nondual *brahman* from ordinary plurality as experienced under *māyā*. Quantum mechanics describes a physical state by a vector or density operator in Hilbert space. For a state

$$\lvert\psi\rangle=\sum_i c_i\lvert i\rangle,$$

measurement of an observable with eigenstates $\lvert i\rangle$ gives the probability

$$P(i)=\lvert c_i\rvert^2.$$

The two accounts have different purposes. Nonduality is a metaphysical and soteriological claim; a quantum superposition is a mathematical state that predicts laboratory statistics. A detector, environment or recorded interaction can be an "observer" in quantum physics. The formalism does not require the observer to be a conscious self.

The useful correspondence is therefore a question: how are apparent distinctions related to an underlying description? The invalid identification is "brahman is the quantum wavefunction." The latter erases the very different meanings and tests of the two concepts.

## Sāṃkhya and physical change

Sāṃkhya distinguishes consciousness (*puruṣa*) from primordial nature (*prakṛti*). Prakṛti evolves through a hierarchy of *tattvas* and is characterized by the three *guṇas*: *sattva*, *rajas* and *tamas*. This supplies a philosophical account of manifestation and experience.

Quantum fields, matter and radiation are defined through measurable degrees of freedom, symmetries and dynamical equations. The three *guṇas* are not three forces, particle types or quantum numbers. A structural analogy may compare an unmanifest-to-manifest sequence with physical emergence, but it does not yield a particle spectrum or a cross-section.

## Cosmological comparison

Modern homogeneous cosmology uses a scale factor $a(t)$. In general relativity its evolution is constrained by a Friedmann equation such as

$$
H^2\equiv\left(\frac{\dot a}{a}\right)^2
=\frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho-\frac{kc^2}{a^2}+\frac{\Lambda c^2}{3}.
$$

This form assumes a homogeneous and isotropic Friedmann--Lemaître--Robertson--Walker spacetime and treats $\rho$ as mass-equivalent density in $\mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}$. Then every term has units $\mathrm{s^{-2}}$. In the convention shown, $k$ is dimensionless and $a$ has units of length; if $a$ is chosen dimensionless, the curvature scale must instead be carried by $k$. The sign of $k$ distinguishes positive, zero and negative spatial curvature, whereas $H>0$ or $H<0$ distinguishes expansion from contraction.

A viable model must specify matter content, curvature and initial conditions, then agree with redshift-distance observations, primordial element abundances and the cosmic microwave background. Indian cyclic cosmologies supply conceptions of vast duration, recurrence and dissolution, but they do not by themselves specify this equation or its observational parameters.

## A test for a genuine correspondence

A proposed correspondence should pass four checks:

1. **Meaning:** define each term in its own textual or experimental setting.
2. **Structure:** state the relation being compared without replacing one vocabulary by the other.
3. **Dynamics:** identify equations, units, signs and limiting cases on the physics side.
4. **Evidence:** ask whether the mapping yields a new, independently testable prediction.

Shared language passes only the first stage. A structural analogy can be intellectually useful without becoming a physical identification; equality of theories would require all four stages.

## What "resonance" can establish

Correspondence can illuminate assumptions. Vedānta can prompt questions about levels of description; Sāṃkhya can sharpen the distinction between consciousness and material process; cyclic traditions can broaden the conceptual space of cosmology. None of these resonances establishes that ancient texts anticipated quantum mechanics or general relativity. Scientific equivalence would require an explicit mathematical mapping and independent predictions, not similarity of words alone.

## Solved Problems

### 1. Born probabilities and a measurable expectation value

A qubit is prepared in

$$
\lvert\psi\rangle=\frac{2\lvert0\rangle+i\lvert1\rangle}{\sqrt5}.
$$

First verify normalization:

$$
\langle\psi\vert\psi\rangle
=\frac{\lvert2\rvert^2+\lvert i\rvert^2}{5}
=\frac{4+1}{5}=1.
$$

Therefore $P(0)=4/5$ and $P(1)=1/5$. For $\sigma_z\lvert0\rangle=+\lvert0\rangle$ and $\sigma_z\lvert1\rangle=-\lvert1\rangle$,

$$
\langle\sigma_z\rangle=P(0)-P(1)=\frac35.
$$

The calculation needs preparation amplitudes and an observable; no claim about a metaphysical self enters the prediction.

### 2. Critical density in a flat, $\Lambda=0$ model

Set $k=0$ and $\Lambda=0$ in the Friedmann equation. The density required by that restricted model is

$$
\rho_c=\frac{3H_0^2}{8\pi G}.
$$

For $H_0=70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$,

$$
H_0=\frac{7.0\times10^4\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}}
{3.0856776\times10^{22}\ \mathrm{m}}
=2.2685\times10^{-18}\ \mathrm{s^{-1}}.
$$

Using $G=6.67430\times10^{-11}\ \mathrm{m^3\,kg^{-1}\,s^{-2}}$ gives

$$
\rho_c=9.20\times10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg\,m^{-3}}.
$$

This is a consequence of stated geometric assumptions, not a number supplied by a philosophical analogy.

## Descriptive Questions

1. Distinguish a shared question, a structural analogy and an identity of scientific theories.
2. Why does the Born rule not require a conscious observer?
3. Explain why the three *guṇas* cannot be identified with three physical forces or quantum numbers.
4. What evidence would be needed to turn a cosmological resemblance into a scientific equivalence?

## Numerical Problems

1. An environmental interaction multiplies an off-diagonal density-matrix element by $0.99$ each time. Find its remaining fraction after $100$ independent interactions.

   **Final answer:** $0.99^{100}=0.366$.

2. Light is emitted when the scale factor is $a_{\rm e}=0.25a_0$. Find its cosmological redshift from $1+z=a_0/a_{\rm e}$.

   **Final answer:** $z=3$.
